The model, input guide & research

What is the predictor?

A transparent pre-match ensemble: surface Elo provides opponent-aware strength; serve/return statistics provide a scoring model. The model does not infer player ability from names, rankings, screenshots or bookmaker odds. No data is fetched automatically.

The bot derives total serve points won from SofaScore rows: 1st-serve-in% × 1st-serve-points-won% + (1 − 1st-serve-in%) × 2nd-serve-points-won%. A direct total serve override still takes priority. Prefer total point counts over an unweighted average of match percentages.

How the calculation works

  1. Match up A’s serving with B’s returning, and vice versa. A’s initial serve-point estimate is the average of A’s serve rate and one minus B’s return rate. This assumes broadly comparable opponent pools; it is not a fitted common-opponent model.
  2. Shrink both serving estimates toward their shared midpoint using n ÷ (n + 10), where n is the smaller surface-match sample. If sample size is missing, use a 0.35 shrinkage weight and force skip.
  3. Calculate advantage-game, tiebreak, set and match probabilities, preserving the server between sets. Average over who serves first. Points are assumed independent with fixed serve strength; no momentum or in-match injuries are modeled.
  4. Convert the Elo difference to a best-of-three probability. For best-of-five, infer a constant set probability and convert to first-to-three sets. Already blended surface Elo is used directly.
  5. Blend the components on the log-odds scale: 60% surface Elo / 40% point model. With only overall Elo, use 45% / 55%. With one component, use that alone and apply stricter evidence checks.
Elo P(A) = 1 / (1 + 10^((EloB − EloA) / 400))
Final P(A) = logistic(w × logit(Elo P) + (1 − w) × logit(Point P))

Confidence and skip policy

A selection needs at least a 64% estimate and must stay at or above 55% in the stress scenarios. The stress check moves each player’s modeled serve-point chance in opposite directions by 1.2 + 4/√n percentage points, shifts the Elo difference by 75 points (100 for overall Elo), and changes the blend weight by 15 percentage points. The displayed range is a sensitivity range, not a statistical confidence interval.

Skip if the fixture is not upcoming, data is stale or incomplete, samples are too small, fitness is unknown, an injury/return or pressure concern is reported, rest is under 18 hours, workload exceeds 240 minutes in 48 hours, or the components materially disagree. A stats-only selection needs at least 20 surface matches per player and comparable opponent levels. Last-10 form is only a cross-check; a large opposing form signal also triggers skip.

“Stronger” confidence needs complete contextual inputs, both models, at least 20 matches each, a 70% central estimate and a 60% stress floor. Otherwise eligible selections receive “Moderate.” All weights, shrinkage amounts, stress sizes and skip thresholds are original design choices, not fitted or validated rules from the cited authors. No profit or win-rate claim is made.

Research and useful data

Jeff Sackmann · Tennis Abstract: An Introduction to Tennis Elo

Explains strength ratings, surface blends, format conversion and Brier-score evaluation. Surface labels hElo, cElo and gElo are already blended with overall strength.

Stephanie Kovalchik (2016) · Searching for the GOAT of tennis win prediction

Compares 11 published forecasting approaches on 2,395 ATP matches. Its results favor careful validation and show that prediction performance differs by player level. This bot does not inherit the study’s accuracy.

Why picking the most winners is not the whole evaluation: probability quality matters too. The results log keeps probabilities so they can be scored later.
